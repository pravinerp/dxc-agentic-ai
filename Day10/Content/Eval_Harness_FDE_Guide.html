Evaluation is not a checkpoint.It's a culture.

An evaluation harness is the repeatable system that produces evidence of AI quality before release and after deployment. What it delivers is a risk-calibrated decision backed by test cases, scores, traces, owners and release controls. A dashboard or a single metric is only a view of that evidence.

The FDE lens

What the harness converts

On a customer site, the harness is the FDE's instrument of proof. It turns four soft things into hard ones.

DemoEvidence

A working demo proves possibility. A scored, versioned run proves reliability for this use case.

OpinionDecision

"It feels better" becomes "v14 beats baseline on three of four gates and is blocked on grounding."

IncidentTest case

Every verified failure becomes a permanent regression test. The same bug never ships twice.

TrustContract

Acceptance gates agreed with the business become the delivery contract and the sign-off record.

Before any architecture

Three questions every FDE must be able to answer

Open a question to see the evidence that answers it.

ReliabilityCan we show the AI system is reliable enough for its specific business use case?
  • Risk-tiered acceptance gates agreed before testing
  • Scorecard segmented by intent and risk, with confidence intervals
  • Comparison against a pinned baseline
  • Adversarial and edge-case coverage, beyond the happy path
OwnershipWho defines acceptable outcomes, owns the ground truth and approves deployment?
  • A named business owner signs the acceptance criteria
  • Domain SMEs maintain a versioned golden dataset
  • Release approvals and overrides are recorded
  • An exception process with a named approver
OperationsHow will quality, unauthorized behavior, cost and latency be detected and managed in production?
  • End-to-end traces with correlation IDs
  • Stratified sampling plus risk-triggered evaluation
  • Alerts that route to an owner and a review queue
  • Incident workflow, SLOs and rollback criteria
