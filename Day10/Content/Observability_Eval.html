Idea 1 · See it

Most agent failures do not crash anything

A normal program fails loudly. An agent fails quietly: it loops, reads too much, picks the wrong tool or is simply slow, and still returns an answer. To find these you need to record every step. That record is a trace.

A healthy run: model, tool, model. Short bars, three rows.
A looping run: the same two bars again and again, each model call a little wider. Nothing crashed.
Say this: "A trace is the flight recorder of an AI agent. When something goes wrong we open it, instead of guessing."
Quick check · Idea 1

Three questions

1. A trace shows the same tool span five times in a row. What is the likely problem?
Repeated identical spans are the signature of a loop.
2. The answer is wrong but nothing crashed. Where do you look first?
Silent failures show up as a wrong or missing step.
3. What can the AWS Bedrock log NOT show?
It sits at the model API. Tools run in your code, so you also trace in the app.
Idea 2 · Afford it

Cost = tokens × price × tickets

You pay for every token the model reads and every token it writes, at every call. An agent makes several calls per question, and each call re-reads the whole history.

Six steps cost far more than six times one step: history is paid for again at every call.

Quick check · Idea 2

Three questions

1. One question made 6 model calls. Why is the cost more than 6 times one call?
Input tokens grow at every step because the history is sent again.
2. You must cut the bill. What is the first thing to try?
Tokens drive the price, so reduce tokens before changing the model.
3. A model is half the price per token but needs twice the steps. Cost per ticket is...
Compare cost per TICKET and pass rate, not the price per token.
Idea 3 · Prove it

An eval is a unit test for AI behaviour

A good demo proves nothing. An eval is a fixed set of questions where each answer is checked for a named risk and marked PASS or FAIL. Run it before every release.

The loop behind every evaluation. You do it in tab 4.
Say this: "We do not say it works because the demo looked good. We say: 14 of 14 on safe writes, 12 of 12 on grounded answers, and here is what we changed when it was not."
Quick check · Idea 3

Three questions

1. Where should your risk categories come from?
Derive them from observed outputs, then check them every release.
2. A developer changes the prompt on Friday evening. What happens before it goes live?
Prompts are code. Same exam, one change, compare.
3. Why is a PASS/FAIL check better than a 1 to 10 score?
A named FAIL tells you exactly what to fix.
Before the lab · How the app works

What happens behind the Ops Control Room

One Streamlit app. Follow the numbers.

The app you will use. Tab 4 (exam) runs the whole line 14 times and checks each run.
  1. You ask a question, or the exam asks 14 of them. app.py
  2. The agent loop: ask the model, run the tool it asks for, ask again. It stops after 6 model calls. run_agent()
  3. The model is Bedrock Nova (or Azure, OpenAI, or the Offline stand-in). model_factory.py
  4. The tools are the same four as Days 5 to 9. The failure switches (stuck, huge, slow) are added in front. askit_core/tools.py
  5. The tracer wraps every model call and tool call in a span with time, tokens and cost. ops_trace.py
  6. Saved in your folder; sent live to Langfuse if you entered the keys. AWS logs the model calls on its own side.
